How the Task Works

Block 1 — Valence Evaluation

Participants see a concept (e.g., Democrats, freedom, universal health care) and press a key to classify it as good or bad. They have 3 seconds to respond. The key assignment is counterbalanced across participants (half press E for "good", half press I for "good").

Each of the participant's 10 nodes appears twice across two sub-blocks. Nodes are drawn from three categories: identity (Democrats, Republicans, women, men, etc.), policy (abortion access, cutting taxes, etc.), and values (freedom, equality, tradition, etc.).

Before the main task, participants must pass a mastery practice: 5 correct responses in a row using unambiguous stimuli (world peace = good, cancer = bad, etc.). If they fail a batch of 5 trials without reaching 5 in a row, they receive feedback and try again. The streak carries across batches.

Block 2 — Pairwise Association

Participants see two concepts side-by-side and press a key indicating whether they go together or do not go together in their mind. They have 4 seconds to respond. As in Block 1, the key assignment is counterbalanced.

Every possible pair among the participant's 10 nodes is tested once; pairs involving me are tested twice (stronger signal for self-concept connections). Trials are split across two sub-blocks to reduce fatigue.

Mastery practice uses unambiguous pairs: ice cream + summer = together, fire + ice = apart, etc. Same 5-in-a-row rule applies.

Node Selection

Each participant sees 10 nodes: me (always included) plus 9 randomly sampled from the full pool of 24. The pool spans:

  • Identity (10): Democrats, Republicans, White/Black people, women, men, working/middle/poor class people
  • Policy — morality (3): abortion access, same-sex marriage, capital punishment
  • Policy — economy (3): cutting taxes, raising the minimum wage, universal health care
  • Policy — non-partisan (3): paying college athletes, disaster relief funding, regulating AI companies
  • Values (5): freedom, equality, tradition, security, compassion

How the Measures Are Calculated

Valence Score (from Block 1)

For each node, we compute how often a participant responded "good" (prop_good), then weight that by how quickly they responded relative to their own average response time. Fast responses signal more automatic, confident evaluations.

# Within-person RT normalization
rt_ratio = node_median_rt / person_median_rt_b1
rt_factor_b1 = max(0.4, 1 − 0.3 × rt_ratio)

# Valence score (0 = strongly bad, 0.5 = neutral, 1 = strongly good)
valence_centered = (prop_good − 0.5) × rt_factor_b1
valence_score = 0.5 + valence_centered

A rt_ratio of 1.0 means the person responded at their average speed → rt_factor = 0.70. Twice as fast (ratio = 0.5) → rt_factor = 0.85. Twice as slow (ratio ≥ 2.0) → floor of 0.40. This is averaged across participants and sub-blocks to get the population-level valence for each node.

Association Strength (from Block 2)

For each pair of nodes, assoc_strength = |prop_together − 0.5|. A pair strongly classified as "together" (prop_together = 0.9) and a pair strongly classified as "apart" (prop_together = 0.1) both get strength = 0.4. Direction (together vs. apart) is tracked separately as centered_assoc = prop_together − 0.5.

RT-Weighted Edge Strength

Faster responses indicate more automatic, implicit associations. We weight each edge by within-person RT to give faster responses more influence in the final network.

# For each participant × pair
rt_ratio = pair_median_rt / person_median_rt_b2

# Averaged across participants, then:
rt_factor = max(0.4, 1 − 0.3 × mean_ratio)
rt_weighted_strength = assoc_strength × rt_factor

Both "go together" and "do not go together" responses contribute — what matters is that the person was fast and consistent, not which direction they leaned.

Centrality

Two centrality measures are computed for each node using rt_weighted_strength as edge weights:

  • Weighted strength: sum of all incident edge weights — how strongly a node is connected overall.
  • Eigenvector centrality: how connected a node is to other highly-connected nodes — the "hub of hubs" measure.

Both are normalized to [0, 1] within each sample.

Network Layout

The Fruchterman-Reingold force-directed algorithm places nodes. Edges exert attractive force proportional to rt_weighted_strength, so strongly and quickly associated concepts cluster together. Me receives extra gravitational pull toward the center. In the interactive view you can drag nodes, zoom, and pan to explore the structure.

Running the Analysis in R

After running the experiment (or the simulator), download the CSV files and place them in a simulated_data/ folder next to belief_network.R. Then open the R script in RStudio and source it. Required files:

  • block1_evaluation_summary.csv — per-participant × per-node valence data
  • block2_pair_summary.csv — per-participant × per-pair association data

The script produces five PDF plots in a plots/ subfolder:

  • 01_full_network.pdf — full belief network, all participants
  • 02_rt_scatter.pdf — RT vs. association strength scatter
  • 03_association_heatmap.pdf — pairwise association matrix
  • 04_group_networks.pdf — Liberal / Centrist / Conservative subnetworks
  • 05_me_connections.pdf — self-concept (me) connections

It also saves plots/node_centrality.csv with strength and eigenvector scores for every node.
